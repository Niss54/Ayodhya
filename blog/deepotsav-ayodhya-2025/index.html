Deepotsav 2025 Ayodhya — Complete Travel Guide

This grand Ayodhya Diwali celebration transforms the sacred city into a mesmerizing realm of light, devotion, and spirituality. Millions of flickering diyas illuminate the ghats, echoing the joyous return of Lord Ram, Sita Mata, and Lakshman after their 14-year vanvas. Deepotsav is not just a festival; it is a profound spiritual yatra that draws devotees and tourists from across the globe.

1. What Is Deepotsav & Why Is It World Famous?

Deepotsav, literally translating to the "Festival of Lights," is a majestic celebration organized annually by the Uttar Pradesh government to recreate the Treta Yug magic of Diwali in Ayodhya. Over the years, this divine event has gained global recognition, primarily for setting successive Guinness World Record milestones for lighting the largest number of oil lamps simultaneously. In 2025, the administration aims to light up lakhs of diyas (millions of earthen lamps) across the city's 51 ghats, with the epicenter being the iconic Ram Ki Paidi.

But Deepotsav is far more than a record-breaking spectacle. It is a soulful expression of faith. The entire city is adorned with vibrant rangolis, floral arches, and laser shows depicting scenes from the Ramayana. For a true bhakt, walking the parikrama paths illuminated by diyas brings an immense sense of peace. The atmosphere is filled with the continuous chanting of Vedic mantras, the fragrance of sandalwood and incense, and the collective devotion of millions. The celebrations also include grand cultural processions, featuring folk artists from various states of India performing traditional dances and Ramlila enactments. You can witness beautifully crafted floats depicting the life of Lord Ram parading through the main streets, creating a festive fervor unlike any other. For travelers, witnessing Deepotsav is a once-in-a-lifetime tirtha that connects you deeply with India's spiritual roots.

2. Expected Dates & Main Events in 2025

Planning your pilgrimage requires knowing the precise Deepotsav dates 2025. Typically, the mega Deepotsav event takes place on Choti Diwali, which falls exactly one day before the main Diwali festival. In 2025, Diwali is expected to be celebrated around late October or early November (exact dates depend on the lunar calendar). The Deepotsav festivities, however, span over three to five days, turning the entire week into a grand spiritual carnival.

The main events usually begin with a grand Shobha Yatra (procession) in the morning, featuring magnificent tableaux starting from Saket College and concluding at Ram Katha Park. Here, artists dressed as Lord Ram, Sita, and Lakshman arrive on a symbolic Pushpak Viman (usually a decorated helicopter), and the Chief Minister performs their symbolic Rajyabhishek (coronation). As dusk approaches, the focus shifts to Ram Ki Paidi diyas. Volunteers meticulously arrange and light lakhs of diyas along the steps. The synchronized lighting is followed by a spectacular projection mapping and laser show on the facades of the temples lining the ghat.

The evening culminates with a grand Saryu Maha Aarti, where priests perform the ritual with massive multi-tiered brass lamps. 3. Best Viewing Spots Along Saryu River

With millions flocking to the city, finding a good vantage point to witness the Deepotsav Ayodhya 2025 illuminations requires strategic planning. The absolute center of attraction is Ram Ki Paidi, where the Guinness World Record is attempted. However, this area is highly restricted and crowded. Only authorized volunteers, dignitaries, and early arrivals get close to the steps.

Pro Travel Tip

To secure a good spot along the ghats, you must arrive by 3PM. The security cordons usually restrict entry to the main viewing areas by 4PM or 5PM.

If you miss out on Ram Ki Paidi, do not worry; the entire stretch of the Saryu Ghat is illuminated. Naya Ghat and Guptar Ghat offer slightly less crowded but equally mesmerizing views of the river adorned with floating lamps. Many devotees prefer taking a boat ride on the Saryu River before sunset to get a panoramic view of the illuminated ghats from the water. Watching the reflection of lakhs of diyas shimmering on the sacred waters is an ethereal experience. For those who cannot make it to the ghats early, the laser show and fireworks can be enjoyed from various rooftops and elevated vantage points across the city. After the main events, taking a peaceful walk to offer your seva, partake in the temple bhog, and receive divine prasad adds a blissful end to the day.

4. How to Book Hotels During Deepotsav

Finding accommodation during the Ayodhya Diwali celebration is notoriously difficult. As the event draws massive global and domestic crowds, hotels, dharamshalas, and guesthouses fill 2-3 months in advance. The demand is so extraordinarily high that room prices surge 3-4x during the festival week. A standard room that usually costs ₹1,500 might easily touch ₹5,000 to ₹7,000 per night.

To avoid last-minute disappointments and exorbitant prices, advance booking is strictly necessary. We advise locking in your accommodation by July or August if you plan to visit in October or November. When choosing a hotel, look for properties located near the central areas like Naya Ghat or Ram Janmabhoomi, as traffic restrictions make long-distance travel within the city nearly impossible on the main day.

5. Transport & Getting Around During the Festival

Navigating Ayodhya during Deepotsav requires patience and a solid understanding of the local traffic management plan. During the peak festival days, the city administration enforces strict traffic diversions and multi-tiered security cordons. Heavy vehicles are restricted from entering the city limits, and four-wheelers are usually not allowed near the main mandir corridors or the Saryu Ghat area.

If you are traveling from Lucknow, Varanasi, or Gorakhpur, you will likely be directed to designated parking zones on the outskirts of the city. From these parking areas, the administration provides e-buses and e-rickshaws to ferry passengers to the central zones. Walking is often the most reliable mode of transport for the last mile, so be prepared for significant walking and wear comfortable footwear.

When is Deepotsav 2025 in Ayodhya?

Deepotsav is typically celebrated on Choti Diwali, which is one day before the main Diwali festival. For 2025, it is expected to fall in late October or early November. We recommend checking the official lunar calendar updates or contacting Mahakaal Tours for exact dates.

How many diyas will be lit during Deepotsav 2025?

The administration aims to break its previous Guinness World Record by lighting over 25 lakh (2.5 million) diyas across 51 ghats, with the main concentration at Ram Ki Paidi.

Yes, entry is free, but access is highly restricted due to massive crowds and security. You must arrive by 3PM to secure a good viewing spot before the main ghats are cordoned off.
